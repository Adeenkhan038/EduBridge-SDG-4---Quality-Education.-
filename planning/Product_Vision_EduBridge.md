Title: EduBridge – SDG 4 Quality Education
 “Connecting rural learners with university STEM mentors, one focused session at a time.”

 ## Background and Problem Statement
 In many rural secondary schools, high school students who are passionate about STEM fields have been found to have markedly poor access to qualified peer tutors outside normal hours of operation. They are often forced to rely on overburdened teachers, general instructional videos, or autonomous study, which has greatly affected their ability to ask questions, clear misconceptions, or receive feedback relevant to their curriculum.From the perspective of the students, the distance between their town and city, the cost associated with traveling to the city, and the financial situation of the student have all contributed to the impracticality of the normal tutoring centers. Moreover, the nature of the devices, which are mostly low-end smartphones, has greatly affected the students’ ability to fully engage with the video sessions or the intensive nature of the applications.


## Target Users and Stakeholders
Primary users – Rural high‑school students
Aged roughly 15–19, living in rural or semi‑rural areas with limited access to STEM tutoring.
Need simple, low‑data access to patient tutors who understand their syllabus and exam format.
Secondary users – University student volunteers
STEM undergraduates who want to contribute to educational equity, build mentoring skills, and gain community‑service experience.
Have limited time, need flexible scheduling, and require a safe, structured way to interact with school students.
Supporting stakeholders:School coordinators and teachers who want visibility into which students are receiving help, what subjects are covered, and whether support aligns with curriculum goals.

What each group wants from EduBridge
Students: easy way to find a suitable STEM tutor, request help, and feel more confident for exams.
Tutors: a clear platform to list availability, manage sessions, and track their volunteering impact.
Schools: oversight of tutoring activities and assurance that interactions are appropriate and aligned with learning goals.

## Vision of the Solution
EduBridge has designed a mobile web platform that will securely link rural secondary high school students with university STEM peer tutors to facilitate short, focused one-on-one peer tutoring sessions. The user experience will be characterized by a text-based interface with minimal data consumption, with the aim of enabling the student to browse the available peer tutors, request a session, and receive support without the need to install large applications or engage in long video interactions.

## Key Product Objectives
- Improve the accessibility and personalization of support to secondary high school students who, in most cases, lack reliable peer tutoring support.
- Allow university students to volunteer their time and expertise in a flexible manner, using the peer tutoring platform.
- Allow the school and the coordinating organizations to have basic visibility into the peer tutoring activities, such as subject areas and scheduling.
- Improve the confidence and motivation levels of the students in the subject area, thus enabling them to ask questions and receive timely feedback.

## High‑Level Features (Scope for Prototype)
Student enrollment and basic student profile (name, institution, education level, and basic subjects in STEM disciplines).
Tutor profile, including their expertise in subjects, a short bio, university affiliation, and basic windows of general availability.
Students ability to browse and filter tutors by subjects, education level, and language, along with a basic overview of their general availability.
Submission or scheduling of a one-on-one instructional session, including preferred time window and short description of the topic or question.
Session history for both student and tutor, showing upcoming and completed sessions in a straightforward and chronological manner.
Basic confirmation and reminder process, including simple status indicators such as “requested,” “accepted,” and “completed.”
Simple feedback or rating mechanism for student perceptions and for tutor reflection.
Lightweight coordinator interface (even mock-up), showing students who have tutors and subjects being addressed.

## SDG 4 Alignment and Justification
EduBridge directly and significantly impacts the achievement of Sustainable Development Goal 4 (SDG 4), which seeks to address barriers to inclusive and equitable quality education and learning opportunities for all. This is especially true for rural high school students who are currently underserved for quality access to high-quality tutoring for STEM courses. EduBridge removes these barriers through its service, providing a low-cost and low-data-channel approach for academic support, leading to improved foundational STEM learning, better examination preparedness, and improved self-confidence for pursuing higher learning.
Removal of barriers for access to quality STEM tutoring for rural high school students is essential for creating greater equity in education by reducing the disparity between those who have and those who do not have tuition budgets, yet have equal potential. The use of university volunteers as peer tutors is practically applicable for this purpose, especially because it is encouraged and supported by universities and students want to enrich their curriculums vitae, improve communication skills, and give back to their rural high school communities in a safe and structured environment.


## Success Criteria (How We Will Know It Works)
In the user tests, the rural students are able to utilize the prototype to find at least one appropriate tutor for the selected STEM subject without assistance. The feedback from the students and the tutors shows that the process of browsing, requesting, and confirming the tutoring session is clear and not confusing. The school coordinators or the instructors are able to understand the clear overview screen (or mock-up), which displays the assignments of the students to the tutors and the sessions scheduled. The users describe the interface as “simple” or “easy to use” on basic smartphones, even with slower network speeds.

##  Constraints and Assumptions
Prototype Characteristics: The prototype does not include real authentication, message encryption, and the integration of video and voice features. The user flows have been conceptualized and include only click-through functionality. The design follows a mobile-first strategy and assumes that the user has access to a minimum smartphone with a current browser and possibly intermittent network connectivity. The pages have been required to be lightweight, text-based, with minimal images and no substantial downloads. The university volunteers have been assumed to be pre-approved by the school or university coordinator before they are integrated into the system.The tutoring sessions are assumed to be conducted using other means not covered by the prototype (WhatsApp, basic telephone calls), with the focus of the EduBridge platform on the process, not the session itself.
Development follows Agile/Scrum practices with two 1‑week sprints (Sprint 1: design and early prototype; Sprint 2: refinement and user testing), tracked in Trello and GitHub to demonstrate iterative progress and teamwork
