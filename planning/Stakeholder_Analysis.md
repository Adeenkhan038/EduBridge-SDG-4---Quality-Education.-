1. Rural High‑School Students (Primary Users)

    Who they are
    Form 4–5 students in rural secondary schools who struggle with STEM subjects and have limited access to tuition.

    Interests / Needs
     -Personalised explanations for exam topics.
     -A safe, simple way to reach tutors using a basic smartphone and limited data.
     -Confidence that the tutor understands the national syllabus.

    Influence on the project
    Very high – the whole system is designed around their learning experience. If their needs are not met, EduBridge fails its SDG 4 goal.

    How EduBridge addresses them
     -Allows them to browse suitable tutors by subject and level.
     - Provides an easy booking flow for 1‑to‑1 sessions.
     -  Keeps the interface light and mobile‑friendly for weaker connections.

2. University Student Volunteers (Primary Users)

    Who they are
    Undergraduate students (STEM‑related courses) who want to tutor secondary school students as a form of community service or portfolio building.

    Interests / Needs
     - A structured way to offer help without handling everything manually.
     -  Clear information about the student’s level and topics before a session.
     -   Simple scheduling that fits around their own classes and part‑time work.

    Influence on the project
    High – without enough tutors, the platform cannot offer meaningful support to students.

   

    How EduBridge addresses them
      - Lets them create tutor profiles and select subjects they can teach.
      - Provides a simple availability setting and session list.
      - Gives them a record of sessions that can be used as volunteering evidence.

3.School Coordinators / Teachers (Key Stakeholders)

  Who they are
    Teachers or heads of department in rural schools who manage student performance and run extra‑help programmes.

  Interests / Needs
     -Assurance that all tutors are appropriate and sessions are safe.
     -Visibility of which students are receiving tutoring and in which subjects.
     -Simple reports to share with principals or education officers.

   Influence on the project
    Very high – they decide whether the school adopts EduBridge and they act as the bridge between students and external tutors.

   How EduBridge addresses them
     -Includes a role for approving and managing tutor accounts.
     -Provides an overview of scheduled and completed sessions.
     -Offers basic summary information (e.g., number of sessions per subject).

4. School Administrators and Principals (Supporting Stakeholders)

    Who they are
    School leaders responsible for overall academic performance and external programmes.

    Interests / Needs
     - Evidence that EduBridge improves students’ results or engagement.
     - Low‑risk, low‑cost initiatives aligned with national education goals and SDG 4.
     -  Assurance that the programme does not overload teachers.

    Influence on the project
    Medium–high – they support or block the use of EduBridge at school level.

    How EduBridge addresses them
      -Produces simple participation summaries they can review.
      -Demonstrates that most coordination happens online, reducing manual admin.
